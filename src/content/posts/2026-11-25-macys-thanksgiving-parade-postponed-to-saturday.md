The Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade won't march on Thanksgiving this year.

New York City officials, the NYPD, Macy's and NBC agreed Wednesday to push the 100th edition of the parade to Saturday, Nov. 28. Step-off is 8:30 a.m. Eastern, which is 7:30 a.m. in Wisconsin. It is the first time the parade has ever been postponed.

The reason is wind. City emergency management officials expect gusts of 50 to 60 mph on Thursday, with sustained winds of 30 to 38 mph and moderate to heavy rain. The National Weather Service has issued high wind and coastal flood warnings for New York from Thursday into Friday. Strong winds are the long-standing worry with the parade's giant helium balloons, which can be pushed off course and into the crowds lining the route.

"The safety of our participants and spectators is our top priority," Macy's said in a statement.

Balloon crews were already inflating characters on Manhattan's Upper West Side when the call came.

Viewers who tune in Thursday morning won't come away empty-handed. NBC will air a rerun of the 2025 parade in its usual Thanksgiving slot. Last year's broadcast set a viewership record of about 34.3 million people across NBC and Peacock.

The parade has been called off only three times, from 1942 to 1944, during World War II. Weather grounded its balloons once, in 1971. It has never before been moved to another day.

Wisconsin has no band in this year's lineup. That changes next year: the UW-Eau Claire Blugold Marching Band has been invited to march in the 2027 parade, which will make it the ninth Wisconsin ensemble to appear in the event's history.
